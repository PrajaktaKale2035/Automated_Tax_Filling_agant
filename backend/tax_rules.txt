Tax Rules for FY 2024-25 (AY 2025-26) - Indian Income Tax (ITR-1 / Sahaj)

1. Income Tax Slabs (New Regime, default for FY 2024-25):
   The new regime is the default tax regime for individuals filing ITR-1.
   - Up to Rs 3,00,000: Nil
   - Rs 3,00,001 to Rs 7,00,000: 5%
   - Rs 7,00,001 to Rs 10,00,000: 10%
   - Rs 10,00,001 to Rs 12,00,000: 15%
   - Rs 12,00,001 to Rs 15,00,000: 20%
   - Above Rs 15,00,000: 30%

2. Income Tax Slabs (Old Regime, optional):
   Taxpayers may opt out of the new regime and choose the old regime to claim
   deductions like 80C / 80D / HRA / LTA. Old regime slabs are stiffer.
   - Up to Rs 2,50,000: Nil
   - Rs 2,50,001 to Rs 5,00,000: 5%
   - Rs 5,00,001 to Rs 10,00,000: 20%
   - Above Rs 10,00,000: 30%

3. Standard Deduction (Salaried & Pensioners):
   - New Regime: Rs 75,000 (raised in Budget 2024 from Rs 50,000).
   - Old Regime: Rs 50,000.
   Applied automatically against gross salary; no proof needed.

4. Section 80C - Investments and Specified Payments (Old Regime Only):
   Maximum deduction Rs 1,50,000 per financial year. Eligible instruments:
   - Public Provident Fund (PPF)
   - Employee Provident Fund (EPF)
   - Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) mutual funds
   - Life Insurance Premium (LIC)
   - 5-year tax-saving fixed deposits
   - National Savings Certificate (NSC)
   - Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana
   - Repayment of housing loan principal
   - Children's tuition fees (up to two children)
   80C is NOT available under the new regime.

5. Section 80D - Health Insurance Premium (Old Regime Only):
   Deduction for medical insurance premiums paid for self, family, and parents.
   - Self, spouse, dependent children: up to Rs 25,000
     (Rs 50,000 if any insured person is a senior citizen, age 60+)
   - Parents: up to Rs 25,000 additional
     (Rs 50,000 additional if parents are senior citizens, age 60+)
   - Preventive health check-up: Rs 5,000 sub-limit within above caps.
   80D is NOT available under the new regime.

6. Section 87A Rebate:
   A tax rebate that reduces tax liability to zero up to a threshold.
   - New Regime (FY 2024-25): rebate of up to Rs 25,000 if total income is up
     to Rs 7,00,000. Effectively makes income up to Rs 7L tax-free.
   - Old Regime (FY 2024-25): rebate of up to Rs 12,500 if total income is up
     to Rs 5,00,000. Effectively makes income up to Rs 5L tax-free.
   Available to resident individuals only (not HUFs / non-residents).

7. Surcharge on Income Tax:
   Additional levy on the tax amount (NOT on income) for high-income earners.
   - 10% surcharge: total income above Rs 50 lakh, up to Rs 1 crore
   - 15% surcharge: total income above Rs 1 crore, up to Rs 2 crore
   - 25% surcharge: total income above Rs 2 crore (new regime cap)
   - 37% surcharge: applies only in the OLD regime above Rs 5 crore
     (the new regime caps surcharge at 25%, no 37% slab).
   Marginal relief is provided so that surcharge does not push net tax above
   the slab boundary by more than the excess income.

8. Health and Education Cess:
   A flat 4% cess is levied on (basic income tax + surcharge), regardless of
   regime, age, or income level. The cess funds national health and primary
   education programs.
   Formula: total tax payable = (slab tax - 87A rebate + surcharge) * 1.04
   Example: if slab tax after rebate is Rs 1,00,000 and surcharge is Rs 0,
   the cess is 4% of Rs 1,00,000 = Rs 4,000, total Rs 1,04,000.

9. TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) and Form 16:
   Form 16 is the salary TDS certificate issued by an employer at year-end.
   It contains:
   - Part A: TAN of employer, PAN of employee, quarterly TDS deposit details.
   - Part B: gross salary, exemptions u/s 10, deductions u/s 16, taxable
     income, slab tax, rebate, cess, total tax, TDS deducted.
   When filing ITR-1, the TDS shown in Form 16 / Form 26AS is credited
   against the computed tax liability. Excess TDS results in a refund;
   shortfall results in tax due.

10. PAN and Aadhaar Linkage:
    - PAN (Permanent Account Number): 10-character alphanumeric ID, mandatory
      for filing ITR. Format: 5 letters + 4 digits + 1 letter (e.g. ABCDE1234F).
    - Aadhaar: 12-digit unique ID issued by UIDAI. Mandatory to link with PAN
      for ITR filing; ITR-1 is rejected if PAN-Aadhaar link is missing.

11. ITR-1 (Sahaj) Eligibility:
    ITR-1 is the simplest form, available only to resident individuals with:
    - Total income up to Rs 50 lakh
    - Income from salary / pension, one house property, other sources
      (interest, family pension), and agricultural income up to Rs 5,000
    NOT eligible if you have capital gains, business income, multiple house
    properties, foreign income/assets, or are a director in a company.

12. HRA (House Rent Allowance) Exemption - Old Regime Only:
    Salaried employees in rented accommodation claim HRA exemption under
    Section 10(13A). The exemption is the MINIMUM of three values:
    - Actual HRA received from employer
    - 50% of basic salary (metro cities: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai)
      OR 40% of basic salary (all non-metro cities)
    - Actual rent paid minus 10% of basic salary
    HRA exemption is NOT available under the new regime.
    Mandatory: rent receipts; landlord PAN required if annual rent > Rs 1,00,000.
    If employee owns a house in the same city, HRA exemption is disallowed.

13. Income from House Property (Allowed in ITR-1 - One Property Only):
    ITR-1 permits income from one self-occupied OR one let-out property.
    Self-Occupied Property:
    - Annual value treated as NIL.
    - Home loan interest deductible up to Rs 2,00,000 under Section 24(b)
      in OLD regime. Under new regime this deduction is NOT available.
    Let-Out Property:
    - Gross annual rent is taxable income under "House Property".
    - Standard deduction of 30% of net annual value allowed for repairs.
    - Full home loan interest deductible (no upper cap) under Section 24(b).
    NOT eligible for ITR-1: two or more house properties (must use ITR-2).

14. Savings Bank Interest and Section 80TTA / 80TTB - Old Regime Only:
    Interest earned on savings bank accounts, FDs, and RDs is taxable as
    "Income from Other Sources". Deductions available in OLD regime:
    - Section 80TTA: Deduction up to Rs 10,000 on savings bank interest only.
      Available to individuals and HUFs below age 60.
    - Section 80TTB: Deduction up to Rs 50,000 on ALL interest income
      (savings bank + fixed deposits + recurring deposits).
      Available ONLY to SENIOR CITIZENS (age 60 and above).
    FD interest is NOT covered by 80TTA; it is fully taxable for non-seniors.
    80TTA and 80TTB are NOT available under the new regime.

15. Regime Comparison - When to Choose Old vs New (FY 2024-25):
    The new regime is the DEFAULT for FY 2024-25; opt-out explicitly for old.
    New regime advantages:
    - Lower slab rates; standard deduction Rs 75,000 (vs Rs 50,000 old).
    - Tax-free up to Rs 7,00,000 income due to 87A rebate (Rs 25,000).
    - Simpler; no need to maintain investment proofs.
    Old regime is beneficial when total deductions exceed roughly Rs 3,75,000:
    - 80C up to Rs 1,50,000 (PPF, ELSS, LIC, EPF, etc.)
    - 80D up to Rs 25,000 - Rs 1,00,000 (health insurance)
    - HRA exemption (varies by salary and rent)
    - Home loan interest up to Rs 2,00,000 under 24(b)
    - 80TTA/80TTB (savings / FD interest deduction)
    Break-even rule of thumb: if total deductions > Rs 3,75,000, old regime
    saves more tax for incomes above Rs 10 lakh. Below Rs 7 lakh, new regime
    is almost always better because of the Rs 25,000 full rebate under 87A.

16. ITR-1 Filing Deadline and Late Filing Penalty (AY 2025-26):
    Original due date: July 31, 2025 (for FY 2024-25 / AY 2025-26).
    Belated return: Filed after July 31 but before December 31, 2025.
    Late filing fee under Section 234F:
    - Rs 1,000 if total income is up to Rs 5,00,000.
    - Rs 5,000 if total income exceeds Rs 5,00,000.
    Interest under Section 234A: 1% per month on unpaid tax from due date.
    Revised return: Allowed until December 31, 2025 to correct mistakes.
    e-Verification: Mandatory within 30 days of filing using Aadhaar OTP,
    net banking, DEMAT account, or send signed ITR-V to CPC Bengaluru.
    Unverified returns are treated as NOT filed.

17. Advance Tax (Applicable when Tax Due > Rs 10,000):
    If total tax liability minus TDS exceeds Rs 10,000, advance tax is mandatory.
    Installment schedule (percentage of total annual tax liability):
    - 15% by June 15
    - 45% by September 15
    - 75% by December 15
    - 100% by March 15
    Salaried employees whose employer deducts 100% TDS are usually exempt.
    Interest under Section 234B: 1% per month if advance tax < 90% of liability.
    Interest under Section 234C: 1% per month on shortfall at each installment.
    Advance tax is the same for both old and new regime.
