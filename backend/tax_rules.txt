Tax Rules for FY 2024-25 (AY 2025-26) - Indian Income Tax (ITR-1 / Sahaj)

1. Income Tax Slabs (New Regime, default for FY 2024-25):
   The new regime is the default tax regime for individuals filing ITR-1.
   - Up to Rs 3,00,000: Nil
   - Rs 3,00,001 to Rs 7,00,000: 5%
   - Rs 7,00,001 to Rs 10,00,000: 10%
   - Rs 10,00,001 to Rs 12,00,000: 15%
   - Rs 12,00,001 to Rs 15,00,000: 20%
   - Above Rs 15,00,000: 30%

2. Income Tax Slabs (Old Regime, optional):
   Taxpayers may opt out of the new regime and choose the old regime to claim
   deductions like 80C / 80D / HRA / LTA. Old regime slabs are stiffer.
   - Up to Rs 2,50,000: Nil
   - Rs 2,50,001 to Rs 5,00,000: 5%
   - Rs 5,00,001 to Rs 10,00,000: 20%
   - Above Rs 10,00,000: 30%

3. Standard Deduction (Salaried & Pensioners):
   - New Regime: Rs 75,000 (raised in Budget 2024 from Rs 50,000).
   - Old Regime: Rs 50,000.
   Applied automatically against gross salary; no proof needed.

4. Section 80C - Investments and Specified Payments (Old Regime Only):
   Maximum deduction Rs 1,50,000 per financial year. Eligible instruments:
   - Public Provident Fund (PPF)
   - Employee Provident Fund (EPF)
   - Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) mutual funds
   - Life Insurance Premium (LIC)
   - 5-year tax-saving fixed deposits
   - National Savings Certificate (NSC)
   - Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana
   - Repayment of housing loan principal
   - Children's tuition fees (up to two children)
   80C is NOT available under the new regime.

5. Section 80D - Health Insurance Premium (Old Regime Only):
   Deduction for medical insurance premiums paid for self, family, and parents.
   - Self, spouse, dependent children: up to Rs 25,000
     (Rs 50,000 if any insured person is a senior citizen, age 60+)
   - Parents: up to Rs 25,000 additional
     (Rs 50,000 additional if parents are senior citizens, age 60+)
   - Preventive health check-up: Rs 5,000 sub-limit within above caps.
   80D is NOT available under the new regime.

6. Section 87A Rebate:
   A tax rebate that reduces tax liability to zero up to a threshold.
   - New Regime (FY 2024-25): rebate of up to Rs 25,000 if total income is up
     to Rs 7,00,000. Effectively makes income up to Rs 7L tax-free.
   - Old Regime (FY 2024-25): rebate of up to Rs 12,500 if total income is up
     to Rs 5,00,000. Effectively makes income up to Rs 5L tax-free.
   Available to resident individuals only (not HUFs / non-residents).

7. Surcharge on Income Tax:
   Additional levy on the tax amount (NOT on income) for high-income earners.
   - 10% surcharge: total income above Rs 50 lakh, up to Rs 1 crore
   - 15% surcharge: total income above Rs 1 crore, up to Rs 2 crore
   - 25% surcharge: total income above Rs 2 crore (new regime cap)
   - 37% surcharge: applies only in the OLD regime above Rs 5 crore
     (the new regime caps surcharge at 25%, no 37% slab).
   Marginal relief is provided so that surcharge does not push net tax above
   the slab boundary by more than the excess income.

8. Health and Education Cess:
   A flat 4% cess is levied on (basic income tax + surcharge), regardless of
   regime, age, or income level. The cess funds national health and primary
   education programs.
   Formula: total tax payable = (slab tax - 87A rebate + surcharge) * 1.04
   Example: if slab tax after rebate is Rs 1,00,000 and surcharge is Rs 0,
   the cess is 4% of Rs 1,00,000 = Rs 4,000, total Rs 1,04,000.

9. TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) and Form 16:
   Form 16 is the salary TDS certificate issued by an employer at year-end.
   It contains:
   - Part A: TAN of employer, PAN of employee, quarterly TDS deposit details.
   - Part B: gross salary, exemptions u/s 10, deductions u/s 16, taxable
     income, slab tax, rebate, cess, total tax, TDS deducted.
   When filing ITR-1, the TDS shown in Form 16 / Form 26AS is credited
   against the computed tax liability. Excess TDS results in a refund;
   shortfall results in tax due.

10. PAN and Aadhaar Linkage:
    - PAN (Permanent Account Number): 10-character alphanumeric ID, mandatory
      for filing ITR. Format: 5 letters + 4 digits + 1 letter (e.g. ABCDE1234F).
    - Aadhaar: 12-digit unique ID issued by UIDAI. Mandatory to link with PAN
      for ITR filing; ITR-1 is rejected if PAN-Aadhaar link is missing.

11. ITR-1 (Sahaj) Eligibility:
    ITR-1 is the simplest form, available only to resident individuals with:
    - Total income up to Rs 50 lakh
    - Income from salary / pension, one house property, other sources
      (interest, family pension), and agricultural income up to Rs 5,000
    NOT eligible if you have capital gains, business income, multiple house
    properties, foreign income/assets, or are a director in a company.

12. HRA (House Rent Allowance) Exemption - Old Regime Only:
    Salaried employees in rented accommodation claim HRA exemption under
    Section 10(13A). The exemption is the MINIMUM of three values:
    - Actual HRA received from employer
    - 50% of basic salary (metro cities: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai)
      OR 40% of basic salary (all non-metro cities)
    - Actual rent paid minus 10% of basic salary
    HRA exemption is NOT available under the new regime.
    Mandatory: rent receipts; landlord PAN required if annual rent > Rs 1,00,000.
    If employee owns a house in the same city, HRA exemption is disallowed.

13. Income from House Property (Allowed in ITR-1 - One Property Only):
    ITR-1 permits income from one self-occupied OR one let-out property.
    Self-Occupied Property:
    - Annual value treated as NIL.
    - Home loan interest deductible up to Rs 2,00,000 under Section 24(b)
      in OLD regime. Under new regime this deduction is NOT available.
    Let-Out Property:
    - Gross annual rent is taxable income under "House Property".
    - Standard deduction of 30% of net annual value allowed for repairs.
    - Full home loan interest deductible (no upper cap) under Section 24(b).
    NOT eligible for ITR-1: two or more house properties (must use ITR-2).

14. Savings Bank Interest and Section 80TTA / 80TTB - Old Regime Only:
    Interest earned on savings bank accounts, FDs, and RDs is taxable as
    "Income from Other Sources". Deductions available in OLD regime:
    - Section 80TTA: Deduction up to Rs 10,000 on savings bank interest only.
      Available to individuals and HUFs below age 60.
    - Section 80TTB: Deduction up to Rs 50,000 on ALL interest income
      (savings bank + fixed deposits + recurring deposits).
      Available ONLY to SENIOR CITIZENS (age 60 and above).
    FD interest is NOT covered by 80TTA; it is fully taxable for non-seniors.
    80TTA and 80TTB are NOT available under the new regime.

15. Regime Comparison - When to Choose Old vs New (FY 2024-25):
    The new regime is the DEFAULT for FY 2024-25; opt-out explicitly for old.
    New regime advantages:
    - Lower slab rates; standard deduction Rs 75,000 (vs Rs 50,000 old).
    - Tax-free up to Rs 7,00,000 income due to 87A rebate (Rs 25,000).
    - Simpler; no need to maintain investment proofs.
    Old regime is beneficial when total deductions exceed roughly Rs 3,75,000:
    - 80C up to Rs 1,50,000 (PPF, ELSS, LIC, EPF, etc.)
    - 80D up to Rs 25,000 - Rs 1,00,000 (health insurance)
    - HRA exemption (varies by salary and rent)
    - Home loan interest up to Rs 2,00,000 under 24(b)
    - 80TTA/80TTB (savings / FD interest deduction)
    Break-even rule of thumb: if total deductions > Rs 3,75,000, old regime
    saves more tax for incomes above Rs 10 lakh. Below Rs 7 lakh, new regime
    is almost always better because of the Rs 25,000 full rebate under 87A.

16. ITR-1 Filing Deadline and Late Filing Penalty (AY 2025-26):
    Original due date: July 31, 2025 (for FY 2024-25 / AY 2025-26).
    Belated return: Filed after July 31 but before December 31, 2025.
    Late filing fee under Section 234F:
    - Rs 1,000 if total income is up to Rs 5,00,000.
    - Rs 5,000 if total income exceeds Rs 5,00,000.
    Interest under Section 234A: 1% per month on unpaid tax from due date.
    Revised return: Allowed until December 31, 2025 to correct mistakes.
    e-Verification: Mandatory within 30 days of filing using Aadhaar OTP,
    net banking, DEMAT account, or send signed ITR-V to CPC Bengaluru.
    Unverified returns are treated as NOT filed.

17. Advance Tax (Applicable when Tax Due > Rs 10,000):
    If total tax liability minus TDS exceeds Rs 10,000, advance tax is mandatory.
    Installment schedule (percentage of total annual tax liability):
    - 15% by June 15
    - 45% by September 15
    - 75% by December 15
    - 100% by March 15
    Salaried employees whose employer deducts 100% TDS are usually exempt.
    Interest under Section 234B: 1% per month if advance tax < 90% of liability.
    Interest under Section 234C: 1% per month on shortfall at each installment.
    Advance tax is the same for both old and new regime.

18. Section 80CCD(1B) - National Pension System (NPS) Additional Deduction:
    Additional deduction up to Rs 50,000 for own contributions to an NPS Tier-1 account.
    This is OVER AND ABOVE the Rs 1,50,000 ceiling of Section 80C, so the total
    deduction possible in old regime: 80C (Rs 1.5L) + 80CCD(1B) (Rs 50K) = Rs 2,00,000.
    Available to salaried employees, self-employed, and government employees.
    80CCD(1B) is OLD regime only. NOT available under the new regime.

    Section 80CCD(2) is different - it covers EMPLOYER contribution to NPS:
    - Up to 10% of (basic salary + DA) for private-sector employees
    - Up to 14% of (basic salary + DA) for central/state government employees
    80CCD(2) is the ONLY Chapter VI-A deduction available under the new regime.

19. Section 80G - Donations to Charitable Organizations (Old Regime Only):
    Deduction for donations to specified institutions, funds, and trusts.
    Categories of donations:
    - 100% deduction without limit: PM National Relief Fund, National Defence Fund,
      PM CARES Fund, Swachh Bharat Kosh, Clean Ganga Fund.
    - 100% deduction subject to 10% of Adjusted Gross Total Income cap.
    - 50% deduction without limit: Indira Gandhi Memorial Trust, Rajiv Gandhi Foundation.
    - 50% deduction subject to 10% of AGTI cap: most charitable trusts under Section 12A.
    Cash donations exceeding Rs 2,000 are NOT deductible. Use bank transfer, cheque, or UPI.
    Donor must obtain a stamped receipt with the institution's PAN and 80G registration number.
    80G is NOT available under the new regime.

20. Leave Travel Allowance (LTA) - Section 10(5) Exemption (Old Regime Only):
    Exemption for actual travel cost incurred for self and family on leave within India.
    Coverage limited to: actual fare for the shortest route. NOT covered: hotel, food,
    local conveyance, or any non-travel expenses.
    Frequency: TWO journeys allowed in a block of FOUR calendar years.
    Current block: 2022-2025 (calendar years). Previous block: 2018-2021.
    Mode-wise limits:
    - Air travel: economy class fare on a national carrier (e.g. Air India)
    - Rail: AC First Class fare for the shortest route
    - Road (where rail is not connected): equivalent first class / deluxe bus fare
    Travel must be within INDIA only. International travel is NOT covered.
    Family = spouse, children, dependent parents, dependent siblings.
    LTA exemption is NOT available under the new regime.

21. Professional Tax - Section 16(iii) (Old Regime Only):
    Professional tax is levied by state governments on salaried individuals.
    Maximum Rs 2,500 per year (varies by state - common in Maharashtra, Karnataka,
    West Bengal, Tamil Nadu; not levied in Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh).
    Employer deducts it monthly and pays to the state government.
    Appears as a deduction line in Form 16 Part B.
    Under OLD regime: deductible from gross salary under Section 16(iii).
    Under NEW regime: Section 115BAC allows ONLY Section 16(ia) standard deduction.
    Section 16(ii) entertainment allowance and 16(iii) professional tax are NOT
    deductible in the new regime.

22. Gratuity and Leave Encashment Exemptions - Section 10(10) and 10(10AA):
    Available in BOTH old and new regimes (these are exemptions from income, not
    Chapter VI-A deductions).

    Gratuity (Section 10(10)):
    - Government employees: fully exempt without any limit.
    - Private sector covered by Payment of Gratuity Act 1972: exempt up to LEAST of:
      a) Actual gratuity received
      b) Rs 20,00,000 (lifetime cap across all employers)
      c) (Last drawn salary x 15/26) x years of completed service
    - Private sector NOT covered by the Act: exempt up to LEAST of:
      a) Actual gratuity received
      b) Rs 20,00,000
      c) (Average 10-month salary x 1/2) x years of completed service

    Leave Encashment (Section 10(10AA)):
    - Government employees: fully exempt without limit.
    - Private sector employees on retirement / resignation: exempt up to LEAST of:
      a) Actual leave encashment received
      b) Rs 25,00,000 (raised from Rs 3,00,000 in May 2023, applies FY 2023-24 onwards)
      c) Average 10-month salary
      d) Cash equivalent of unutilised earned leave (max 30 days per year of service)

    Excess above the exemption limit is taxable under "Income from Salary".

23. Senior Citizen and Super Senior Citizen Slabs (Old Regime Only):
    The OLD regime has age-based basic exemption thresholds:
    - Below 60 years: Rs 2,50,000 nil slab
    - Senior Citizen (age 60-79): Rs 3,00,000 nil slab
    - Super Senior Citizen (age 80+): Rs 5,00,000 nil slab
    Age determined as of last day of the financial year (March 31, 2025 for FY 2024-25).

    The NEW regime has the SAME slab structure for all ages:
    - Up to Rs 3,00,000: Nil (slab applies regardless of age)
    Age-based benefits do NOT apply in the new regime. Senior citizens choosing
    the new regime do not get a higher nil slab.

    Super senior citizens (80+) with no business income are also EXEMPT from advance
    tax obligations under Section 207, regardless of regime.

24. Section 87A Marginal Relief - Edge Case Around the Threshold:
    The 87A rebate creates a tax cliff: just above the rebate threshold the tax
    jumps sharply. Marginal relief smooths this discontinuity.

    New Regime FY 2024-25 example:
    - Total income exactly Rs 7,00,000: rebate Rs 25,000 -> total tax Rs 0
    - Total income Rs 7,00,001: NO rebate -> slab tax Rs 25,000 + 4% cess = Rs 26,000
    - Without marginal relief, Rs 1 of additional income would create Rs 26,000 of tax.

    Marginal relief rule: tax payable shall not exceed the income above the threshold.
    For total income Rs 7,00,001, tax payable is capped at Rs 1 (the excess above Rs 7L).

    Marginal relief applies only up to the break-even point where slab tax naturally
    equals the income excess (around total income Rs 7,27,777 in new regime FY 2024-25).
    Above the break-even, normal slab tax applies and no marginal relief is given.

    Old Regime: same principle applies around the Rs 5,00,000 / Rs 12,500 rebate threshold.

25. Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS:
    Form 26AS - tax credit statement showing:
    - All TDS deducted by employers, banks, tenants
    - Advance tax and self-assessment tax paid
    - Refunds issued in the year
    - Specified high-value financial transactions
    Available at: https://www.incometax.gov.in -> Services -> View Form 26AS

    AIS - Annual Information Statement (introduced FY 2021-22, more comprehensive):
    Shows ALL financial transactions reported to the Income Tax Department:
    - Salary, interest, dividends from all sources
    - Mutual fund and share transactions
    - Property purchases and sales above thresholds
    - Foreign remittances, large credit-card spends
    Cross-reference AIS with Form 16 and bank statements before filing.
    If AIS shows incorrect data, submit feedback through the AIS portal.
    Pre-filled ITR-1 fetches data from 26AS and AIS automatically.

26. New Regime - Complete List of Disallowed Deductions and Exemptions:
    The following are NOT available under Section 115BAC (new regime, default for AY 2025-26):

    Salary-side (Sections 10 and 16):
    - HRA exemption (Section 10(13A))
    - LTA exemption (Section 10(5))
    - Children's education and hostel allowances
    - Entertainment allowance (Section 16(ii))
    - Professional tax (Section 16(iii))

    Chapter VI-A deductions:
    - 80C (PPF, ELSS, LIC, NSC, EPF, etc.)
    - 80CCC and 80CCD(1) (own pension contributions)
    - 80CCD(1B) (additional NPS Rs 50K)
    - 80D (health insurance)
    - 80E (education loan interest)
    - 80G (donations)
    - 80TTA / 80TTB (savings / FD interest)
    - 80U (disability)
    - All other Section 80 deductions

    House Property:
    - Section 24(b) interest deduction for self-occupied house: NOT available
    - 30% standard deduction on let-out property: AVAILABLE (part of HP income computation)

    What IS allowed under the new regime:
    - Standard deduction Rs 75,000 (Section 16(ia))
    - 80CCD(2) - employer's NPS contribution (10% private / 14% govt of basic+DA)
    - 80JJAA - employment-generation deduction (only for businesses, not ITR-1)
    - Family pension standard deduction Rs 25,000 (Section 57(iia))
    - Rebate u/s 87A (Rs 25,000 if total income <= Rs 7L)
    - Surcharge cap at 25% (vs 37% in old regime above Rs 5cr)

27. ITR-1 Form Structure - Schedules and Parts:
    The ITR-1 (Sahaj) form is organised into the following sections:

    Part A - General Information:
    - Name, PAN, Aadhaar, address, email, mobile
    - Filing status (resident, individual or HUF)
    - Employer category (government, PSU, private, pensioner)
    - Tax regime selection (new is default; opt for old explicitly)
    - Original or revised return; original date and acknowledgement if revised

    Schedule S - Income from Salary:
    - Gross salary as per Form 16
    - Allowances exempt under Section 10 (HRA, LTA - only if old regime)
    - Standard deduction (auto-filled Rs 75K new / Rs 50K old)
    - Professional tax (only old regime)
    - Net taxable salary

    Schedule HP - Income from House Property (one property only):
    - Address and ownership percentage
    - Self-occupied vs let-out
    - Annual value, municipal taxes, 30% standard deduction (let-out only)
    - Interest on home loan under Section 24(b)

    Schedule OS - Income from Other Sources:
    - Savings bank interest
    - Fixed deposit and recurring deposit interest
    - Family pension (with applicable standard deduction)
    - Other interest income

    Schedule VI-A - Deductions (old regime only):
    - 80C, 80D, 80G, 80CCD(1B), 80E, 80TTA / 80TTB, etc.

    Part B-TI - Total Income computation
    Part B-TTI - Tax on Total Income (slabs, rebate, surcharge, cess)
    Schedule TDS - TDS from Form 16 and bank TDS (cross-checked with 26AS)
    Schedule IT - Advance and self-assessment tax paid

28. e-Verification Methods After Filing:
    Filing is INCOMPLETE without e-verification. Unverified ITR is treated as not filed.
    Time limit: 30 days from date of filing (reduced from 120 days in 2022).

    Methods (in order of speed and convenience):
    1. Aadhaar OTP - Aadhaar must be linked to PAN. OTP sent to Aadhaar-linked mobile.
       Fastest method (under 2 minutes).
    2. Net Banking - Login through bank's net banking portal -> Income Tax e-filing link.
       Generates EVC (Electronic Verification Code). Most major banks supported.
    3. DEMAT account - EVC via CDSL or NSDL pre-validated DEMAT account.
    4. Bank ATM - EVC via select bank ATMs (SBI, Axis, Canara, ICICI, etc.).
    5. Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) - mandatory for businesses, optional for ITR-1.
    6. Offline (last resort) - Print signed ITR-V, post by ordinary or speed post to:
       Centralized Processing Center, Income Tax Department, Bengaluru - 560500.
       Must reach CPC within 30 days of filing date.

    Bank account pre-validation is required to receive any refund. Link bank account at
    the e-filing portal under Profile -> My Bank Account.
    PAN-Aadhaar must be linked before filing or the ITR will be rejected.

29. Budget 2024 Key Changes (FY 2024-25 / AY 2025-26):
    Changes effective for ITR-1 filings under AY 2025-26:

    New regime (default regime from AY 2024-25):
    - Standard deduction RAISED from Rs 50,000 to Rs 75,000 for salaried and pensioners.
    - Family pension standard deduction RAISED from Rs 15,000 to Rs 25,000 (Section 57).
    - New regime tax slabs revised (see section 1).
    - 87A rebate: Rs 25,000 if total income <= Rs 7,00,000 (unchanged from FY 2023-24).
    - Surcharge in new regime continues to cap at 25% (no 37% slab even above Rs 5cr).

    Old regime:
    - Standard deduction unchanged at Rs 50,000.
    - Slabs unchanged.
    - 87A rebate: Rs 12,500 if total income <= Rs 5,00,000 (unchanged).
    - Surcharge can reach 37% above Rs 5cr.

    NPS:
    - 80CCD(2) employer contribution limit RAISED from 10% to 14% of (basic + DA) for
      central and state government employees. Private sector employer cap remains 10%.

    Filing-side improvements:
    - New regime is the DEFAULT. To choose old regime, the taxpayer must opt out
      explicitly in the ITR-1 form (Part A, "Tax regime selection").
    - Pre-filled ITR enhanced - more data auto-populated from AIS, 26AS, and Form 16.

## Tax Slabs for AY 2026-27***
New Tax Regime Vs Old Tax Regime: The Finance Act 2023 has amended the provisions of Section 115BAC w.e.f AY 2024-25 to make new tax regime the default tax regime for the assessee being an Individual, HUF, AOP (not being co-operative societies), BOI and or Artificial Juridical Person. However, the eligible taxpayers have the option to opt out of default tax regime and choose to be taxed under old tax regime. The old tax regime refers to the system of income tax calculation and slabs that existed before the introduction of the new tax regime. In the old tax regime, taxpayers have the option to claim various tax deductions and exemptions. However, in default tax regime, tax rates are lower with very few deductions compared to old tax regime. In "Non-business cases", option to change the default tax regime can be exercised every year directly in the ITR and such ITR is required to be filed on or before the due date specified under section 139(1)of the Income Tax Act,1961. In case of eligible taxpayers having income from business and profession, if assessee wants to opt out of default tax regime, (New Tax regime),they have to furnish Form-10-IEA on or before the due date u/s 139(1)of the Income Tax Act,1961for furnishing the return of income. Also, for the purpose of withdrawal of such option i.e. re-entering into default tax regime (New Tax regime) shall also be done by way of furnishing Form No.10-IEA on or before the due date specified u/s 139(1)of the Income Tax Act,1961for furnishing return of income. However, option to withdrawfrom theold tax regime and re-enter into default tax regime (New Tax regime) ,is available only in subsequent AY and is available only once in lifetime for eligible taxpayers having income from business and profession.
